## What's new in v0.8.3

This is the one that explains why automatic mode felt erratic. It was not slow, and it was not a
setting. **It was translating the sentence one word short, and then refusing to translate the
finished one.**

### The defect

To decide a line has stopped changing, the app reads it twice and compares. It asked the wrong
question first.

It asked *"are these the same words?"* before *"is this the same line with more on the end?"* — and
the first test allows up to three characters of difference, which is exactly what a sentence one
character short of finished looks like. So the half-written line was accepted as final, translated,
and saved permanently. Then the complete sentence arrived a moment later and was thrown away as a
duplicate of the fragment.

Measured against four real lines from the game: **every one was released one character early.**

That is the worst answer this app can give — a fluent, confident, wrong Arabic sentence, shown to
somebody who cannot check it against the English. And it explains the shape of the complaint exactly:
whether it bit depended on where the line's typing-out happened to be when two readings landed, so
some lines were perfect and others quietly wrong or missing.

The comparison now asks about shape first, and only then about degree.

### And a long line was dropped for being long

While a sentence was still appearing, each reading was counted as a *failure to agree*. So a line
that took more than four readings used up its budget, gave up, and started its entire three-second
wait over from the beginning. The longer the sentence, the likelier it was to be abandoned — worst
case around nine seconds, and it scaled with how much there was to say.

Waiting for a line to arrive is no longer counted as failing to read it. A separate time limit stops
a screen that grows forever from holding things up.

### Three more, found on the way

- **The Diagnostics tab could crash the app during a run.** It reads a number the watching loop is
  writing at the same moment — in the one screen you would open precisely because something already
  seemed wrong. The same class of fault was also losing increments from the request count the
  session limit is measured against, so the app was under-reporting what it had spent.
- **Switching auto-watch off during a translation could throw**, out of the one part of the app whose
  entire job is never to throw.
- **Taking a snip mid-translation could file that translation under the wrong Arabic style**, which
  makes the saved copy unreachable from then on.

Switching game or profile now also clears what the second reader remembers, which it never did.

---

### What was deliberately not done

A rewrite that keeps watching the screen while a line is being translated was designed, reviewed
hard, and then **not shipped**.

Reviewed properly it introduced more ways to lose or duplicate a line than it removed, and it was
worth about a fifth of a second. The blind spot it closes only costs lines when text changes faster
than roughly one a second — faster than dialogue is read — while the two defects above cost lines at
every length of sentence. It stays on the list, with every problem found in review written down
against it, because it is the right change eventually and the wrong change to ship untested.
